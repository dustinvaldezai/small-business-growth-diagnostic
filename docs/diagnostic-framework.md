# Diagnostic Framework

This framework organizes a growth problem into questions that can be answered with different evidence types.

## 1. Baseline

Questions:

- What is the current monthly operating baseline?
- Are the underlying records comparable across months?
- What does the source data actually represent: accepted, booked, completed, or paid activity?
- Which historical periods are credible comparison points?

Output: a working baseline with explicit caveats.

## 2. Capacity

Questions:

- What volume would the stated target require in service hours?
- How much recurring provider coverage exists?
- Is the bottleneck total headcount or the timing and service compatibility of coverage?
- Which capacity assumptions are observed versus modeled?

Output: scenario ranges, not a forecast.

## 3. Customer behavior

Questions:

- What share of identifiable customers return?
- How quickly do first-observed customers return?
- How much current volume comes from customers with an earlier observed visit?
- Where does observed history begin, and how does that affect interpretation?

Output: directional retention and repeat-behavior signals.

## 4. Conversion and demand

Questions:

- Is customer demand actually insufficient?
- Is the booking path measured well enough to diagnose conversion?
- Are marketing conclusions supported by attribution data, or are they hypotheses?

Output: supported finding, open measurement gap, or hypothesis.

## 5. Intervention logic

Before recommending a tactic, ask:

1. Is the constraint supported by current evidence?
2. Can the business operationally absorb the proposed improvement?
3. Is there a lower-risk measurement or test that should happen first?
4. What would leadership need to see to proceed, adjust, or stop?

This changes the final deliverable from a list of ideas into a decision framework.
