# Assumptions and Limitations

A useful diagnostic should make its uncertainty visible.

## Accepted appointments are a proxy

The operating baseline uses accepted appointments because that was the available working measure. Accepted appointments are **not** the same as verified completed services, so the baseline should not be treated as a perfect completion count.

## The stretch target is not self-validating

A target of 660 monthly appointments was useful as a historical benchmark and planning reference, but the diagnostic did not assume that every strategy should optimize toward it.

## Capacity is scenario-based

Modeled capacity changes with provider-days per week and productive hours per provider-day. The range is intended to test feasibility, not predict exact output.

Service compatibility, peak-time coverage, breaks, room constraints, cancellations, and provider availability can all reduce usable capacity.

## Customer history is incomplete

Observed customer history begins in July 2024. A person appearing as a first-observed customer may have visited before that history window.

Identity matching and event deduplication can also affect counts, which is why retention findings are presented as directional.

## Conversion is under-measured

The available evidence did not establish a full booking-funnel conversion rate. That means the responsible conclusion is that conversion measurement is incomplete, not that the website or booking path is definitively broken.

## Causality is limited

This diagnostic identifies patterns, constraints, and testable hypotheses. It does not prove that any one factor caused historical changes in appointment volume.

## Leadership validation remains part of the process

The analysis ends with a business validation step: does the diagnosis match operating reality, what is missing, and what should be tested next?

That is a feature of the method, not an analytical weakness.
