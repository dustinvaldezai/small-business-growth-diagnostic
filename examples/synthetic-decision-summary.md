# Synthetic Decision Summary

This example shows how diagnostic findings can be translated into a leadership-ready decision without jumping directly to tactics.

## What appears supported

- The business has operated above the current baseline before.
- Available staffing may support more volume under some recurring coverage scenarios.
- Repeat customers contribute meaningfully to current demand.
- A relatively small share of first-observed customers return within 90 days.

## What is not yet proven

- that raw headcount is the primary growth constraint
- that more advertising is the missing solution
- that the booking path is definitively broken
- that replacing the booking platform should be the first intervention
- that a stretch target is automatically the right operating goal

## Measurement gaps

- completed versus accepted appointment status
- full booking-funnel conversion
- peak-time utilization by service type
- economics of additional volume

## Leadership decision

Before approving tactics, confirm three things:

1. Does this diagnosis match day-to-day operating reality?
2. Which assumption or measurement gap would most change the decision?
3. Which small test would reduce uncertainty without creating unnecessary cost or disruption?

## Recommended next-stage structure

Prioritize a small set of workstreams around:

- attracting new customers
- keeping and reactivating existing customers
- improving referrals and booking conversion
- aligning capacity and economics

Then define owners, measures, guardrails, and decision thresholds for any test.

This example is synthetic. It demonstrates the decision logic used in the public case study rather than reproducing a client deliverable.
